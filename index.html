Quick Answer: GLP-1 in Denver

The Science, Simply Explained

What Is GLP-1?

GLP-1 is a hormone your gut naturally releases after eating that helps regulate appetite and blood sugar. GLP-1 medicines mimic and amplify this signal.

Appetite Signals

GLP-1 acts on receptors in the brain areas that regulate hunger and fullness. Many people taking a GLP-1 medicine report thinking about food less and feeling satisfied with smaller portions.

Slower Stomach Emptying

GLP-1 slows how quickly the stomach empties after a meal, so fullness lasts longer and blood sugar rises more gradually after eating.

Glucose-Dependent Insulin Release

GLP-1 prompts the pancreas to release insulin when blood sugar is high. Because this effect depends on glucose levels, it is one reason these medicines were first developed for type 2 diabetes.

Dual-Action Tirzepatide

Tirzepatide acts on both the GLP-1 receptor and the GIP receptor, a second gut-hormone pathway. Semaglutide acts on the GLP-1 receptor only.

Important: These are long-term prescription medicines with real side effects and dosing schedules. Diet, activity and follow-up care still matter. Talk to a licensed clinician about whether a GLP-1 medicine fits your situation.

Step by Step

Finding a Licensed Colorado Prescriber

Whether you see a clinician in a Denver office or by telehealth, the same basic checks apply.

1

Start With Your Own Clinician

Your primary care provider, an endocrinologist or an obesity medicine specialist already knows (or can learn) your history and can tell you whether a GLP-1 medicine makes sense.

2

Verify the License

Check that the prescriber holds an active Colorado license using the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) online license lookup.

3

Expect a Real Evaluation

A legitimate prescriber asks about your medical history, current medications and goals, and may order lab work before writing a prescription.

4

Use a Licensed Pharmacy

Fill your prescription at a pharmacy licensed by the Colorado State Board of Pharmacy (or licensed in your state for mail order), and plan for regular follow-up.

What the Research Showed

Average Weight Loss in the Trials

Published trial averages; individual results vary. Sources: STEP-1 (NEJM 2021, semaglutide 2.4 mg, 68 weeks), SURMOUNT-1 (NEJM 2022, tirzepatide 15 mg, 72 weeks).

How to Read These Numbers

Context matters more than the headline figure.

They Are Averages

Some participants lost much more and some lost little. An average does not predict what will happen for any one person.

Separate Trials

STEP-1 and SURMOUNT-1 enrolled different people at different times, so the two bars are not a direct head-to-head comparison.

Lifestyle Support Was Included

Participants in both trials also received diet and activity counseling, and the results were measured over more than a year of treatment.

FDA-Approved Brands

GLP-1 Medicines Explained

All of these require a prescription from a licensed clinician. Your prescriber decides which, if any, fits your health history and diagnosis.

GLP-1 Receptor Agonist
Semaglutide Injection
Brands: Ozempic® and Wegovy®

The most widely studied GLP-1 medicine. Ozempic is approved for type 2 diabetes; Wegovy is approved for chronic weight management. In the STEP-1 trial, the Wegovy dose produced about 15% average body weight loss over 68 weeks.

  • Once-weekly injection under the skin
  • Dose is increased gradually over several weeks
  • Prescription required
Dual GLP-1 + GIP Agonist
Tirzepatide
Brands: Mounjaro® and Zepbound®

Tirzepatide acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. Mounjaro is approved for type 2 diabetes; Zepbound is approved for chronic weight management. In SURMOUNT-1, the highest dose produced about 20% average body weight loss over 72 weeks.

  • Once-weekly injection under the skin
  • Dose is increased gradually over several weeks
  • Prescription required
GLP-1 Receptor Agonist, Oral
Oral Semaglutide
Brand: Rybelsus®

A daily tablet form of semaglutide approved for type 2 diabetes. It has specific instructions about taking it on an empty stomach, so ask your prescriber or pharmacist exactly how to take it.

  • Once-daily tablet
  • Timing with food and water matters
  • Prescription required

Not sure which one applies to you? Talk to a licensed clinician about your health history and goals.

Be an Informed Patient

Questions to Ask & Red Flags

A good GLP-1 prescriber welcomes questions. A risky online seller avoids them.

01

Is a GLP-1 Right for Me?

Ask whether your health history, current medications or family history make a GLP-1 medicine a poor fit, and which brand and dose your prescriber recommends and why.

02

What Side Effects Should I Expect?

Ask which side effects are common, which are warning signs that need a call right away, and how the dose will be increased to reduce stomach upset.

03

What Will It Cost?

Brand-name list prices can exceed $1,000 per month without insurance, and coverage varies. Ask about your plan's coverage, prior authorization and the long-term cost of staying on treatment.

04

Red Flag: No Prescription Needed

Any seller offering GLP-1 medicines without a prescription or a real medical evaluation is not operating legitimately.

05

Red Flag: Unlicensed Pharmacy

If you cannot confirm the pharmacy is state-licensed, or it ships from outside the U.S., do not use it. Your pharmacist should be easy to reach with questions.

06

Red Flag: Too Good to Be True

Very low prices, "research use only" labels, miracle claims or pressure to buy now are all signs to walk away and talk to a licensed clinician instead.

FDA warning: The FDA has warned consumers about counterfeit GLP-1 products and about unapproved or compounded versions sold online. Stick to prescriptions from a licensed clinician, filled by a licensed pharmacy.
